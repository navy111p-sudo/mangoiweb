3Most likely cause [inference]

The office upload capacity is being filled up, so packets queue and delay explodes. Engineers call this “bufferbloat”. The signature is exactly what we measured: delay jumping from 0.06 s to 1.6 s while packet loss stays comparatively low.

A video class has to upload the teacher's camera continuously. Home and small-office plans in the Philippines usually have a much smaller upload than download. Once upload is full, everything on that line — including the class — starts queueing.

Three candidates, and we cannot tell them apart from Korea:

  1. The contracted upload speed is simply too small for the number of simultaneous classes.
  2. Something else on the same line is uploading — cloud backup, Windows Update, file sync, YouTube upload, another video call.
  3. The ISP itself is congested in that time slot.

Ruled out on our side (measured): our relay servers were healthy; the two people were in the same room; there were no ghost connections; the teacher PC no longer records video (we removed that load on 3 Sept morning); and our video quality setting is already on “Low”, so we are not sending a heavy stream.

6What we already did, and what we are building

Done (deploying tonight): the video no longer flickers on and off. Previously the app restored video after only 8 seconds of calm and then dropped it again; now it waits for 32 seconds of stable line before turning the camera back on. In a simulation of exactly your 3 Sept conditions this reduced the switching from 12 times to 1 over four minutes.

Also shipped this week: video quality now recovers on high-latency lines instead of staying blurry; the teacher PC no longer records the class (that load moved off her machine); and a badge now tells everyone when the picture is low quality, so nobody thinks the app is broken.

Building next: we are adding per-line quality tracking. Today our dashboard shows quality per person; it will also group by internet line, so we can see statements like “the office line degrades every day between 14:00 and 16:00”. That gives you a documented, time-stamped record to take to the ISP — which is much stronger than a phone complaint.

To be clear: none of our software changes make the line itself faster. They stop the symptoms from being worse than the line. Sections 4 and 5 are the real fix.
