1 Why this makes video freeze but not voice

The internet has two ways to send things. Think of them as courier and registered mail.

UDP — the courier
If a package is late, it is thrown away and the next one goes through. Video looks a little rougher for a second, then continues. This is what video calls are built for.
TCP — registered mail
Every package must arrive, in order. If one is late, everything behind it waits in line. Video stops dead, then jumps. This is what we are stuck on now.

Voice survives registered mail because it is tiny — about 30 kbps, and the browser can paper over small gaps. Video is roughly 40× bigger. One late packet stalls the whole picture, then a burst arrives. That is exactly the “freeze… jump… freeze” teachers describe.

So the problem is not your internet speed. We measured round-trip times of 113–324 ms during the broken calls — that is a perfectly normal, healthy connection. The traffic is simply being forced down the wrong lane.

3 What we already fixed on our side

We are not asking you to carry this alone. Since 3 September we shipped:

  • Stopped the flicker. When the line got bad, our software switched to audio-only and then tried to come back after only 8 seconds — over and over. That is why the picture kept appearing and vanishing. It now waits 32 seconds of a genuinely stable line before restoring video.
  • Stopped teachers' laptops recording. Every browser in the room was making its own recording, which used the same upload and the same CPU as the live video. Teachers no longer record automatically.
  • Lower start on slow lines. Calls now begin at a lower quality when the line is known to be slow, instead of flooding it and then backing off.
  • We can now see the route. Every call records whether it went direct or through a relay, and over UDP or TCP. That is how we found this — and it is how we will confirm your fix worked.
  • A backup path (server-relayed video). Built and ready, switched off. It helps on connections that can never be opened up — but it is not a substitute for this fix. It is also WebRTC, so where UDP is blocked it falls back to TCP as well.

4 How we will know it worked

You do not have to test anything or report back with numbers. Just tell us the date the change was made. We will read it from our own records.

  1. You change the firewall setting and tell us the date.
  2. Teachers restart their browser and run one normal class.
  3. We look at our route records for that class.
Success looks like this: calls start showing up as direct instead of relay, or at least as relay over UDP instead of TCP — and video freezes drop from 4–9 per minute toward zero.
If nothing changes, that is still useful — it tells us the blocking is at the ISP, not the office router, and we take it up with them next.
